\begin{abstract}
Neural posterior estimators such as \DINGOBNS return binary neutron star (BNS) posteriors in about one second.
Raw samples from the frozen network do not self-diagnose a glitch.
Neural importance sampling can flag a collapsed proposal by a near-zero sampling efficiency~\cite{Dax2023is}, but the $2\times10^{4}$-sample draw that feeds it takes $10.7\,\mathrm{s}$ and the reweighting does not move a posterior that has stuck to the $10\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ prior boundary.
The public GW170817 checkpoint fixes the sky at the optical counterpart, so the comparisons below cover the 15 parameters that checkpoint samples.
No failure occurs below a whitened glitch SNR $\rho_w\approx100$.
Above that, luminosity distance collapses to a prior edge.
The $60\%$ collapse rate above Omicron SNR 300 is $9/15$ Koi-Fish cells from five triggers; $89\%$ of O3 H1 triggers that loud are Extremely Loud and were not tested, so the $11\%$ catalogue tail is not a measured failure rate.
On runs that had already collapsed, the detect--gate--rebuild front-end recovers $17/17$ (Wilson 95\% interval $[81.6\%,100\%]$).
Across all native cells it preserves $181/183=98.9\%$ (cluster bootstrap $[96.7\%,100\%]$; design-effect Wilson $[94.1\%,99.8\%]$), and $60/61$ unique glitches ($98.4\%$; bootstrap $[95.1\%,100\%]$).
The front-end median is $16.9\,\mathrm{ms}$.
Replacing the packaged strain by a fresh \texttt{data\_to\_domain} transform, with or without the $+2\,\mathrm{s}$ cyclic shift, recovers $0/16$ hardening cells; matched-delta with the original ASD recovers $13/16$.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item Conditional recovery on collapsed runs is $17/17$ (Wilson $[81.6\%,100\%]$); $181/183$ cells and $60/61$ unique glitches are preserved.
\item The $11\%$ O3 tail above SNR 300 is $89\%$ Extremely Loud (untested); the $60\%$ collapse there is $9/15$ Koi-Fish cells from five triggers.
\item Raw network samples do not flag glitches; importance sampling diagnoses collapse but does not undo a prior-boundary spike, and a $2\times10^{4}$-sample draw takes $10.7\,\mathrm{s}$.
\item Matched-delta with the original ASD recovers; full DINGO repackaging, shifted or unshifted, recovers none of 16 cells.
\item The front-end median is $16.9\,\mathrm{ms}$ ($19.0\,\mathrm{ms}$ at the 95th percentile). Clean Gaussian false triggers are $0/1000$ at the event threshold.
\end{highlights}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
%% =====================================================================

When two neutron stars merge, the window for finding their electromagnetic counterpart opens immediately and closes within hours.
The kilonova of GW170817 was found because a gravitational-wave sky map and distance estimate reached observers fast enough to tile the right region of the sky~\cite{Abbott2017gw170817,Abbott2017mma,Singer2016bayestar}.
As detector networks approach design sensitivity and binary neutron star (BNS) detections become routine, that latency budget is set by the time needed to turn strain data into a posterior, and conventional stochastic samplers require hours to days for a full BNS analysis~\cite{Morisaki2023rapid,Chaudhary2024latency}.

Amortized neural posterior estimation (NPE) removes this bottleneck by training a conditional density estimator to map strain data to posterior samples in a single forward pass~\cite{Cranmer2020sbi,Papamakarios2016mdn,Greenberg2019snpe,Papamakarios2021review,Durkan2019nsf}.
In gravitational-wave astronomy the programme has progressed from GW150914~\cite{Green2021npe} through DINGO for binary black holes~\cite{Dax2021dingo,Dax2022gnpe,Dax2023is,Wildberger2023psd} to \DINGOBNS~\cite{Dax2025bns}.
The public GW170817 checkpoint used throughout this paper fixes right ascension and declination at the optical counterpart, so the validation covers the 15 parameters that checkpoint samples; all-sky 17-parameter localization is left to future work.
The network is trained on simulated signals in stationary Gaussian noise.

Real interferometer data are not like that.
Transient, non-Gaussian artefacts (glitches) occur often enough that of order one in five compact-binary candidates in the third observing run required some form of glitch mitigation~\cite{Nuttall2018transient,Davis2021detchar,Davis2022subtract,Glanzer2023gravityspy}.
For likelihood-based analyses the effect of a glitch is a bias that can be diagnosed, modelled, and subtracted~\cite{Powell2018glitchpe,Pankow2018mitigation,Payne2022gw200129,Ghonge2024efficacy,Macas2022localization}.
Raw samples from the frozen network do not self-diagnose that input.
Neural importance sampling can, because a proposal that has left the training support receives near-zero weight~\cite{Dax2023is}, but the $2\times10^{4}$-sample draw that feeds it takes $10.7\,\mathrm{s}$ on the hardware used here and does not pull a posterior off a spike at the prior boundary (width $0.65\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ at $10\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ in the control).
The network also cannot be retrained at event time.
The symptom measured below is a luminosity-distance posterior that saturates at a prior edge.
The \DINGOBNS authors themselves note that inference for events overlapping glitches would in practice require fast glitch mitigation~\cite{Dax2025bns}.
Training-time remedies, such as augmenting the training set with glitches or redesigning the density estimator~\cite{Sun2024ttsf,Xiong2025robust}, are valuable but do not protect a checkpoint that is already deployed and whose weights must remain frozen for provenance and operational reasons.

This paper asks two operationally motivated questions.
First, how loud does a glitch have to be before a frozen \DINGOBNS posterior fails, and how often does real detector data cross that line?
Second, can a preprocessing front-end alone, with no change to the network, bring a contaminated input back inside the region where the posterior is trustworthy?
We answer the first with an empirically estimated, morphology-dependent threshold and a catalogue-level rate, and the second in the affirmative, finding that \emph{how} the excised data are written back into the network's input format matters more than the excision itself.
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=0.25em]
\item We place synthetic and real glitches on a common loudness scale (whitened optimal SNR, $\rho_w$) and measure where the frozen network fails: no collapse below $\rho_w\approx100$ in any family, real or synthetic; a collapse onset near $\rho_w\approx200$; a $50\%$ collapse rate that depends on morphology, from $\rho_w\approx5\times10^{2}$ for real Koi-Fish glitches to $\approx3\times10^{3}$ for synthetic sine-Gaussians and broadband bursts; and collapse independent of where the glitch sits within the segment.
These thresholds are empirical estimates for one checkpoint (the official GW170817 demonstration model), one event packaging, and the glitch families and injection conditions used here; we do not claim them as universal properties of \DINGOBNS or of neural posterior estimators.
\item We test 61 real O3 Gravity Spy glitches at native loudness on GW170817: none below Omicron SNR 100 collapses the posterior.
The $60\%$ rate above SNR 300 is $9/15$ Koi-Fish cells from five triggers.
Of confidently classified O3 H1 triggers, $11\%$ exceed that SNR, but $89\%$ of those triggers are Extremely Loud and were not injected here.
\item We introduce a detect--gate--rebuild front-end that leaves the \DINGOBNS weights untouched.
Conditional on an initially collapsed posterior it recovers $17/17$ (Wilson 95\% interval $[81.6\%,100\%]$).
On the full native panel it preserves $181/183$ cells ($98.9\%$; cluster bootstrap $[96.7\%,100\%]$; design-effect Wilson $[94.1\%,99.8\%]$) and $60/61$ unique glitches ($98.4\%$; bootstrap $[95.1\%,100\%]$), and it recovers $86\%$ of a 240-cell loud-glitch grid and $92\%$ of a synthetic BNS panel.
\item Through ablations we show that matched-delta reconstruction with the original ASD is the load-bearing ingredient, and we explain why the difference between it and a naive FFT rewrite is decisive.
\item Comparing detector-driven gates with a fixed-width oracle that knows the glitch time, we show that residual failures are governed by gate extent rather than localization; a clean-gate control quantifies the cost of a false trigger.
\item We release open software, the detector checkpoint, and archived results that reproduce every reported number.
\end{enumerate}

Section~\ref{sec:setup} describes the experimental design.
Section~\ref{sec:results} reports quantitative results.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses mechanism, limitations, and deployment, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:background}
%% =====================================================================

\subsection{Bayesian inference and noise modelling}
\label{sec:bg-bayes}

Standard parameter estimation treats the strain as a stationary Gaussian process and evaluates a Whittle likelihood~\cite{Abbott2020guide,Abbott2019gwtc1,Abbott2021opendata,Abbott2023opendata,Macleod2021gwpy,Ashton2019bilby}.
The PSD choice moves the posterior~\cite{Chatziioannou2019psd,Talbot2020psd}, and a glitch invalidates the interval even when the point estimate looks ordinary~\cite{Edy2021mismodel,Mozzon2020dynamic,Zackay2021nongaussian}.

\subsection{Neural posterior estimation and \DINGOBNS}
\label{sec:bg-npe}

NPE trains $q_\phi(\theta\mid d)$ on simulated pairs $(\theta,d)$~\cite{Cranmer2020sbi,Papamakarios2021review}.
DINGO conditions a flow on whitened frequency-domain strain and the PSD~\cite{Dax2021dingo,Dax2022gnpe}; \DINGOBNS extends that to long BNS signals~\cite{Dax2025bns}.
Importance sampling reweights the draws by the likelihood~\cite{Dax2023is}.
When they sit on a prior boundary the efficiency goes to zero, which is a diagnosis, not a repair: a $2\times10^{4}$-sample draw takes $10.7\,\mathrm{s}$ and a spike one sample wide has nothing else to promote.
A latent PSD model extends the time covered by one network~\cite{Wildberger2023psd}; it does not remove a glitch.

\paragraph{The checkpoint used here}
\label{sec:checkpoint}

Every run uses the official GW170817 demonstration checkpoint~\cite{Dax2025bns}, unmodified, through \texttt{GWSamplerGNPE}.
The training set is $3\times10^{7}$ IMRPhenomPv2-NRTidal injections in stationary Gaussian noise for H1, L1, and V1.
Priors include $m_1\in[1.0,3.2]\,M_\odot$, $m_2\in[1.0,2.0]\,M_\odot$, chirp mass in $[1.0,2.2]\,M_\odot$ with an event-specific half-width of $0.005\,M_\odot$, spins $a_{1,2}\in[0,0.05]$, $\Lambda_1\in[0,5000]$, $\Lambda_2\in[0,10^{4}]$, and $\dL$ uniform on $[10,100]\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
Right ascension and declination are fixed at the optical counterpart ($3.446$ and $-0.408\,\mathrm{rad}$), so the sampler returns 15 parameters and those 15 are what this paper compares.
The package is $T=128\,\mathrm{s}$ at $f_s=4096\,\mathrm{Hz}$, Tukey roll-off $0.4\,\mathrm{s}$, a $2\,\mathrm{s}$ post-trigger buffer, and frequencies from $23$ to $1535.3\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
The Livingston data in that package are the LVK's BayesWave-subtracted frame~\cite{Pankow2018mitigation}.
Injections go into H1.
Dax et al.\ note that a glitch overlapping the event needs a fast mitigation step before this network is used~\cite{Dax2025bns}.

\subsection{Transient noise in ground-based detectors}
\label{sec:bg-glitches}

Glitches have many time--frequency morphologies and were still a leading data-quality issue in early O4~\cite{Abbott2016noise,Nuttall2018transient,Davis2021detchar,Soni2025o4}.
Omicron parametrizes them~\cite{Robinet2020omicron}, iDQ scores them from auxiliary channels~\cite{Essick2021idq}, and Gravity Spy labels the morphology~\cite{Zevin2017gravityspy,Bahaadini2018,Glanzer2023gravityspy}.
Blips and scattered light matter most for compact binaries~\cite{Cabero2019blip,Soni2021scattered}.
About 20\% of O3 candidates needed mitigation, and a nearby glitch moves a low-latency sky map~\cite{Davis2022subtract,Macas2022localization}.

\subsection{Mitigation for likelihood-based parameter estimation}
\label{sec:bg-mitigation}

Likelihood pipelines gate~\cite{Zackay2021nongaussian,Usman2016pycbc,Ewing2024gstlal}, model the glitch with wavelets (BayesWave)~\cite{Cornish2015bayeswave,Cornish2021bayeswave,Pankow2018mitigation,Hourihane2022overlap,Davis2022subtract,Ghonge2024efficacy}, fit a glitch basis~\cite{Merritt2021glitschen,Udall2023scattered,Malz2025joint}, or change the noise model~\cite{Ashton2023gp}.
All of them evaluate a likelihood, and the accurate ones take minutes to hours.

\subsection{Glitches and neural parameter estimation}
\label{sec:bg-neural-glitch}

Two recent lines of work train normalizing flows to remain accurate under glitch contamination, either by time--spectral fusion architectures~\cite{Sun2024ttsf} or by studying robustness to sine-Gaussian contaminants without explicit deglitching~\cite{Xiong2025robust}.
A related data-domain line inpaints sub-second strain gaps: Kumar and Sahay find that global temporal context helps on simulated gaps, while a noise-conditioned network is unstable on real strain unless the conditioning is bounded~\cite{Kumar2026inpaint}.
That result is about reconstructing the missing samples.
The question here is different: once samples have been gated, which frequency-domain convention the frozen estimator will accept.
Malz and Veitch sample a Gravity Spy glitch prior jointly with the signal in Bilby~\cite{Malz2025joint}.
Legin et al.\ put the non-Gaussianity into a score-based likelihood~\cite{Legin2025score}.
Online flows such as AMPLFI see the same data~\cite{Chatterjee2024amplfi}.
On the data side, auxiliary-channel regression, time-domain subtraction, and inpainting of a gated signal are all established~\cite{Cuoco2025review,Ormiston2020deepclean,Dooney2025deepextractor,Mogushi2021nnetfix,Razzano2018cnn,George2018dnn}.
None of them tests how a repaired strain must be written back into a frozen flow.
Here the \DINGOBNS weights stay frozen and only the detector is trained.

\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Problem statement}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $d$ denote the frequency-domain event package consumed by \DINGOBNS (whitened strain and ASDs for each interferometer) and let $q_\phi(\theta\mid d)$ be the frozen neural posterior.
A short glitch $g$ overlapping the analysis segment produces a contaminated package $d_g$ for which $q_\phi(\theta\mid d_g)$ can differ qualitatively from $q_\phi(\theta\mid d)$.
In our diagnostics the dominant symptom is \emph{saturation}: the luminosity-distance marginal, whose clean 90\% interval spans roughly $19$--$45\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ for GW170817, collapses to a narrow spike at one boundary of the network's training prior, $[10,100]\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ (Section~\ref{sec:results-gw170817}).
The network is not returning a biased distance; it is returning the edge of what it is allowed to say, with a credible interval that is far too narrow.
We seek a map $d_g\mapsto \tilde d$ such that $q_\phi(\theta\mid \tilde d)\approx q_\phi(\theta\mid d)$ without modifying $\phi$.

\subsection{A common loudness scale}
\label{sec:loudness}

To compare synthetic injections, real glitches, and catalogue statistics we express every injected glitch $g$ by its whitened optimal signal-to-noise ratio in the injected interferometer,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rhow}
\rho_w^2 = 4\int_{f_{\min}}^{f_{\max}} \frac{|\tilde g(f)|^2}{S_n(f)}\,\mathrm{d}f ,
\end{equation}
evaluated as a discrete sum on the packaged frequency grid ($\Delta f=1/128\,\mathrm{Hz}$) between $f_{\min}=23\,\mathrm{Hz}$ and $f_{\max}=1535.3\,\mathrm{Hz}$, with $S_n$ the square of the H1 analysis ASD that accompanies the GW170817 package.
Here $\tilde g(f)$ is the Fourier transform of the injected time-domain glitch alone, passed through exactly the packaging transform of the event (the $128\,\mathrm{s}$ segment and $0.4\,\mathrm{s}$ Tukey roll-off), so that it is placed on the same grid and in the same conventions as the strain the network sees.
For synthetic families $g$ is the generated waveform; for real Gravity Spy excerpts it is the re-coloured, glitch-only estimate described in Section~\ref{sec:realglitches} after it has been placed in the segment, so $\rho_w$ measures the loudness of what was actually injected.
This is the matched-filter SNR the glitch would have if it were the template, and it is the quantity the whitened network input actually sees.

Gravity Spy reports an Omicron SNR~\cite{Robinet2020omicron} for each trigger~\cite{Zevin2017gravityspy,Glanzer2023gravityspy}, and the two scales are related only approximately, because Omicron matches a sine-Gaussian basis to the trigger while $\rho_w$ integrates whatever power the excerpt contains across the analysis band.
Over the 61 real glitches of the native-loudness panel (Section~\ref{sec:realglitches}), each of which carries both quantities, the ratio $\rho_w/\mathrm{SNR}_{\mathrm{Omicron}}$ has median $1.6$ with a 16--84\% range of $1.0$--$2.3$ and extremes of $0.4$ and $5.4$, and it depends on morphology: median $1.6$ (16--84\% range $1.2$--$1.8$, $n=15$) for blips, $1.4$ ($1.3$--$1.8$, $n=20$) for Koi Fish, $1.3$ ($0.8$--$1.6$, $n=7$) for tomtes, $1.1$ ($0.6$--$2.3$, $n=9$) for scattered light, and $3.0$ ($1.9$--$4.4$, $n=10$) for whistles.
Omicron-SNR bins should therefore be read as approximate loudness bins, accurate to a factor of two or so; wherever real glitches are reported by Omicron-SNR bin (Table~\ref{tab:real}) we also give the median injected $\rho_w$ of that bin, and the catalogue-tail fractions of Section~\ref{sec:results-real} are quoted on the Omicron scale on which the catalogue is defined.
At the Koi-Fish ratio, Omicron SNR 300 corresponds to $\rho_w\approx4\times10^{2}$ and Omicron SNR $2\times10^{3}$ to $\rho_w\approx3\times10^{3}$.

\label{sec:pipeline}

\subsection{STFT glitch detector}
\label{sec:detector}

For each interferometer we form a whitened spectrogram of an analysis crop of $\approx4\,\mathrm{s}$ around the trigger with $n_{\mathrm{fft}}=2048$, a window of $1024$ samples, and a hop of $256$ samples at $4096\,\mathrm{Hz}$; the resulting $1025$ real-FFT frequency bins are linearly interpolated onto $F=128$ bins spanning $20$--$512\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and the time axis is resampled onto $T=32$ bins, so that the detector always sees a fixed $32\times128$ grid.
A compact convolutional network maps the multi-detector spectrogram stack to per-interferometer, per-time-bin logits.
Architecture details and training hyperparameters are given in \ref{app:detector}.
Operating thresholds are calibrated to a target clean false-positive rate of $1\%$ on held-out clean crops; at event time an optional stricter threshold (used in the GW170817 stress grid) further suppresses clean triggers.
Positive time bins are dilated to Tukey windows of half-width $0.4\,\mathrm{s}$ (default).

\subsection{Tukey gating}
\label{sec:gating}

Given gate windows $\{(t_{s,i},t_{e,i})\}$, we form multiplicative weights $w(t)\in[0,1]$ that equal unity outside gates and follow a Tukey taper (shape parameter $\alpha=0.5$) inside each window~\cite{Harris1978windows}.
Gated time-domain strain is $x_{\mathrm{g}}(t)=w(t)\,x(t)$.
Detector-driven gates are built by dilating \emph{every} positive STFT time bin by $\pm0.4\,\mathrm{s}$ and merging overlapping windows, so their total extent grows with the duration of the detected excess.

As a diagnostic we also construct a \emph{centred fixed-width oracle}: a single gate of the same $\pm0.4\,\mathrm{s}$ half-width centred exactly on the true injection time.
The oracle has perfect localization but a fixed extent, so comparing it with the detector-driven gate separates two possible causes of failure, mislocalization and insufficient excision.
It is never used as the primary method.

\subsection{Matched-delta frequency-domain reconstruction}
\label{sec:matched-delta}

Let $X(f)$ be the packaged frequency-domain strain for a gated interferometer and let $\mathcal{F}$ denote the analysis Tukey-windowed real FFT consistent with the \DINGOBNS event packaging.
The matched-delta update replaces $X$ by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:matched-delta}
\tilde X(f) = X(f) + \mathcal{F}[x_{\mathrm{g}}](f) - \mathcal{F}[x](f),
\end{equation}
i.e.\ it adds the Fourier transform of the gated-minus-ungated residual rather than overwriting $X$ with $\mathcal{F}[x_{\mathrm{g}}]$.
If $X=\mathcal{F}[x]$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:matched-delta} reduces to $\tilde X=\mathcal{F}[x_{\mathrm{g}}]$.
The official conditioner is not that bare transform.
\texttt{data\_to\_domain} windows with the $128\,\mathrm{s}$ Tukey ($\alpha=2\times0.4/T$) and then applies \texttt{cyclic\_time\_shift}($+2\,\mathrm{s}$).
On the clean GW170817 strain that single shift matches the archived package to a whitened residual SNR of $0.26$.
Against the windowed FFT without the shift, the same package has relative $L_2$ norm $1.313$ and whitened residual SNR $\rho_\varepsilon=876$; after the $+2\,\mathrm{s}$ phase is restored the relative $L_2$ norm falls below $10^{-12}$.
The $1.313$ figure is that missing shift, not a second shift applied on top of an already shifted strain.
Full replacement throws away $X$ and keeps only the new transform.
Matched-delta adds $\mathcal{F}[x_{\mathrm{g}}]-\mathcal{F}[x]$ onto the original $X$, so any linear convention common to both Fourier transforms cancels and the packaged noise realization is left in place.
Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation} measures the difference: matched-delta recovers $13/16$ hardening cells, and both the shifted and the unshifted full repackages recover none.

\subsection{ASD policy}
\label{sec:asd}

After reconstruction we restore the original analysis ASDs that accompany the event package.
Welch estimates recomputed on the gated segment~\cite{Welch1967} are \emph{not} retained: they encode the gate as a spectral feature and drive residual collapse in ablation.
This choice is the frequency-domain analogue of insisting that whitening statistics match those seen in training~\cite{Chatziioannou2019psd,Talbot2020psd,Wildberger2023psd}.

\subsection{Frozen sampler}
\label{sec:sampler}

Posterior samples are drawn with the official GW170817 \DINGOBNS demonstration checkpoint via the unchanged \texttt{GWSamplerGNPE} interface.
No embedding weights, flow parameters, or PE heads are fine-tuned.
The only learned component contributed here is the STFT detector checkpoint distributed with the accompanying software.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
%% =====================================================================

\subsection{Data and assets}
\label{sec:data}

All GW170817 experiments use the public open-data packaging and the official \DINGOBNS demonstration model~\cite{Abbott2017gw170817,Abbott2021opendata,Abbott2023opendata,Dax2025bns}.
Synthetic BNS events are generated with the same domain conventions (segment duration, sampling rate, frequency grid, and ASD handling) so that the frozen checkpoint remains applicable.
Strain handling uses GWpy and the scientific Python stack~\cite{Macleod2021gwpy,Harris2020numpy,Virtanen2020scipy,Paszke2019pytorch}.

\subsection{Glitch families and severity}
\label{sec:glitches}

We inject morphologically diverse synthetic glitches into a single interferometer (H1 unless noted):
held-in families \{\texttt{sine\_gaussian}, \texttt{broadband\_burst}, \texttt{whistle}, \texttt{scattered\_light}, \texttt{glitch\_train}\} used in detector training, and held-out families \{\texttt{ringing}, \texttt{double\_blip}, \texttt{narrowband\_tone}\}.
Amplitude is set by an in-band amplitude ratio relative to the local raw-strain noise, with severity midpoints $\{3,6,10\}$; \ref{app:glitch} lists parameter ranges.
These three levels were fixed a priori, before the loudness scale of Section~\ref{sec:loudness} existed, to span roughly a factor of three in raw-strain amplitude, an order of magnitude in power, from an excess that is clearly visible above the local noise to one that dominates it; they are retained because the released detector was trained on that distribution and the archived panels were run on it.
The loudness sweep below, not the severity index, is the quantitative axis of this paper.
Because raw in-band RMS is dominated by the low-frequency noise wall, the severity convention produces very loud glitches on the whitened scale: median $\rho_w$ is $4.2\times10^{3}$, $7.3\times10^{3}$, and $1.0\times10^{4}$ at the three severities (range $1.5\times10^{2}$--$6.4\times10^{4}$), and the official-control sine-Gaussian has $\rho_w=4.3\times10^{3}$.
Two facts make the synthetic panels a stress test rather than a population study.
First, their loudness lies one to two orders of magnitude above the bulk of real O3 triggers, whose median Omicron SNR is 13 (Section~\ref{sec:results-real}), and overlaps only the loudest $5$--$10\%$ of the catalogue.
Second, the eight families were chosen for morphological diversity and for held-in/held-out separation, not weighted by how often each morphology occurs in the detector; the panel therefore says nothing about population-averaged failure rates, which is why the native-loudness real-glitch panel and the catalogue-tail fractions are reported separately.
ASD policies alternate between retaining the stationary analysis ASD and recomputing a Welch ASD on the contaminated segment, so that collapse and recovery can be attributed to strain versus noise-spectrum mismodelling.

\paragraph{Loudness sweep}
\label{sec:sweep}
To locate the failure threshold we inject sine-Gaussians and broadband bursts into the GW170817 H1 segment at target $\rho_w$ values log-spaced from $10$ to $10^{4}$ (eight levels, five seeds per family), extended by ten further cells per family at each of $\rho_w\in\{10,30,100\}$ to tighten the low-loudness bound; 139 cells in all, with the injection time drawn uniformly in $[-1.5,-0.3]\,\mathrm{s}$ relative to the trigger.

\paragraph{Real Gravity Spy glitches}
\label{sec:realglitches}
We select H1 triggers from the O3 Gravity Spy classifications~\cite{Glanzer2023gravityspy} with machine-learning confidence $\geq0.95$ and duration $\leq3\,\mathrm{s}$, in four Omicron-SNR bins ($[8,30)$, $[30,100)$, $[100,300)$, $[300,\infty)$) for five morphologies (Blip, Koi Fish, Scattered Light, Tomte, Whistle), taking the highest-confidence triggers in each bin; 72 were selected, 70 fetched from GWOSC (two fell in data gaps), and 61 yielded a usable excerpt (the remaining nine, all quiet scattered-light or tomte triggers, had no whitened excess above the extraction threshold below).
For each, $8\,\mathrm{s}$ of open strain centred on the trigger is whitened with a PSD from its own off-trigger portion, cut to the trigger duration (at least $\pm0.5\,\mathrm{s}$) with a Tukey taper, reduced to a glitch-only estimate by thresholding its STFT at eight times the per-frequency off-trigger median power, re-coloured with the GW170817 H1 analysis ASD, and added to the H1 analysis segment at three injection times per glitch ($183$ cells).
Injection is at \emph{native} loudness, i.e.\ the same whitened energy the glitch had in O3; no rescaling is applied.
Five trigger-free O3 excerpts processed identically (without thresholding) serve as a noise-only control.
Working in the whitened domain is necessary because a Gravity Spy blip at Omicron SNR 20 is not measurable as a raw in-band RMS excess at all.

\subsection{Evaluation panels}
\label{sec:panels}

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=1.2em]
\item[GW170817 stress grid.]
Eight families $\times$ three severities $\times$ two ASD policies $\times$ five seeds yield $N=240$ cells.
A clean reference posterior ($n=512$ samples) anchors recovery criteria.
\item[Synthetic BNS panel.]

\item[Loudness sweep.]

\item[Real Gravity Spy panel.]

\item[Clean-gate cost control.]
$N=25$ forced $\pm0.4\,\mathrm{s}$ gates on glitch-free GW170817 data (20 on H1 at injection times uniform in $[-2.0,-0.2]\,\mathrm{s}$; 5 on H1 and L1 simultaneously), passed through the full front-end and compared with the clean posterior.
\item[Method hardening / ablation.]

$N=1{,}000$ stationary Gaussian draws at thresholds $0.46$ and $0.73$, kept separate from $500$ off-source GWOSC crops.
A no-glitch pass through the front-end is bit-identical to the input when no gate fires.
\end{description}

\subsection{Success criteria}
\label{sec:criteria}

A poisoned posterior is labelled \emph{collapsed} if the 90\% credible interval on $\dL$ has upper edge below $15\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ or lower edge above $90\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ (relative to the GW170817 clean median $\approx 34\,\mathrm{Mpc}$).
The two thresholds correspond to the collapse spikes observed at the prior boundaries.
A gated posterior \emph{recovers} if its $\dL$ median lies in $[20,50]\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ and within $10\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ of the clean median, and its 90\% credible interval overlaps the clean interval.
These are deliberately coarse, $\dL$-only criteria chosen to separate collapse from non-collapse across hundreds of cells with $n=512$ samples each; on successful cells the actual median offsets are far smaller (mean $1$--$1.4\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, reported per panel).
Throughout the paper a posterior that satisfies this criterion is called \emph{recovered}; where the text says a gated posterior is \emph{clean-like}, it means exactly this operational condition and nothing stronger.
The criterion does not by itself establish equivalence of the full posterior.
Parameter-by-parameter Jensen--Shannon divergences are reported for the control (Table~\ref{tab:js}) and, separately, for every initially collapsed real Koi-Fish cell and a synthetic subset (Section~\ref{sec:results-multiparam}).
For that case study we compute Jensen--Shannon (JS) divergences~\cite{Lin1991jsd} between clean and gated one-dimensional marginals for all 15 sampled parameters and require $\mathrm{JS}<0.02\,\mathrm{nat}$ on the core set $\{\Mc,q,\dL,\theta_{JN},t_{\mathrm{c}}\}$.
The threshold was fixed before the comparison was run.
It corresponds to two equal-width Gaussians offset by $0.4\sigma$, a shift well inside any credible interval; it is an order of magnitude below the JS at which two marginals visibly disagree; and it lies above the resampling floor for the sample sizes used in the case study ($n=2\times10^{4}$), so that a pass is informative rather than automatic.
The clean-versus-clean floor at $n=512$ is $0.04\,\mathrm{nat}$ (Section~\ref{sec:results-cleangate}), so the grids stay on the $\dL$ criterion.
The 15-parameter comparison uses $n=10^{4}$ and is reported separately (Section~\ref{sec:results-multiparam}).
All criteria are implemented in the released drivers; the reported rates are read directly from the archived CSVs.

\subsection{Where the frozen network fails}
\label{sec:results-threshold}

Fig.~\ref{fig:threshold} is the loudness sweep on GW170817.
No cell collapses below $\rho_w\approx100$ (89 cells, including 69 at $\rho_w\in\{10,30,100\}$).
The rates are $0\%$ through $\rho_w\approx100$, $10\%$ at $\approx200$, $30\%$ at $\approx500$ and $\approx1400$, $60\%$ at $\approx3700$, and $80\%$ at $10^{4}$.
The $50\%$ point is not a grid point.
Log-linear interpolation between $30\%$ at $\approx1400$ and $60\%$ at $\approx3700$ gives $\rho_w\approx2.7\times10^{3}$ for the two families together, $2.3\times10^{3}$ for broadband bursts, and $6.1\times10^{3}$ for sine-Gaussians, uncertain by about a factor of two.
Real Koi Fish cross $50\%$ several times lower (Section~\ref{sec:results-real}).
At $\rho_w>3000$, pooling this sweep with the stress grid (194 cells), collapse is $56\%$ within $1\,\mathrm{s}$ of the trigger and $53\%$ further away ($p=0.75$).
The front-end recovers $135/139$ cells ($97\%$; $92.8$--$98.9\%$) and $90\%$ of collapsed cells.
Sine-Gaussians recover at every grid point.
Broadband bursts fail only at $\rho_w\approx27$ ($3/5$, over-excision of a harmless glitch; the pooled point is $13/15$) and at $10^{4}$ ($2/5$ still collapsed).
At the extension points the detector fires on $0\%$ of cells at $\rho_w=10$ and about $65\%$ at $\rho_w=30$; recovery is $100\%$ and the mean $\dL$-median offset is $0.5$--$1.6\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.

\subsection{Real O3 glitches at native loudness}
\label{sec:results-real}

Fig.~\ref{fig:real} and Table~\ref{tab:real} are the 183 native-loudness cells.
No cell collapses in the two lower bins (123 cells, 41 glitches, $\rho_w\approx40$--$70$; Wilson $0$--$3.0\%$).
The $[100,300)$ bin collapses $8/45=18\%$ ($9.3$--$31.3\%$, $\rho_w\approx190$) and the $[300,\infty)$ bin $9/15=60\%$ ($35.7$--$80.2\%$, $\rho_w\approx560$).
That last interval is five triggers wide.
Every collapse is a Koi Fish: $53\%$ of Koi-Fish cells at SNR $100$--$300$, $60\%$ above $300$, and $17/60$ overall.
Blips, whistles, tomtes, and scattered light do not collapse at the loudness they reach here, so the Koi-Fish $50\%$ point sits near $\rho_w\approx5\times10^{2}$, about five times below the synthetic crossing.
The five noise-only controls do not collapse and all five are recovered.
The detector fires on them because the transplanted excerpt is a real excess; the clean-data rate is measured on unmodified draws (Section~\ref{sec:results-cleangate}).

The headline is the conditional result: every one of the 17 initially collapsed cells is recovered ($17/17$; Wilson 95\% interval $[81.6\%,100\%]$).
Across all 183 cells the front-end preserves $181/183=98.9\%$.
Those cells are three placements of 61 triggers, so a binomial interval overstates the information.
Resampling triggers gives a cluster-bootstrap 95\% interval $[96.7\%,100\%]$; a design-effect Wilson interval at $n_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx91.5$ (ICC $0.50$) is $[94.1\%,99.8\%]$.
Strict recovery, all three placements of a trigger, is $60/61=98.4\%$ (bootstrap $[95.1\%,100\%]$).
The loudest bin is $15/15$ cells from five triggers (Wilson $[79.6\%,100\%]$).
The mean $\dL$-median offset on successes is $1.0\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
The two failures are both scattered-light cells whose poisoned posterior was already fine and where the gate over-excised, pulling the $\dL$ median to $18$ and $22\,\mathrm{Mpc}$; the centred oracle, with its narrower support, recovers both.
The detector fires on $87\%$ of cells overall, rising from $85\%$ in the quietest bin to $100\%$ above SNR 300; the cells it does not fire on are quiet ones that pass through unchanged and, being non-collapsed, count as recovered.
The centred oracle recovers $90\%$, losing to the detector on every morphology, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:results-oracle}.

\paragraph{Prevalence in the O3 catalogue}
\label{sec:prevalence}
Among the $132{,}065$ O3 H1 Gravity Spy triggers with confidence $\geq0.95$, $11.0\%$ exceed SNR $300$.
That tail is not a Koi-Fish tail.
Extremely Loud accounts for $14{,}894$ triggers and $89\%$ of the triggers above SNR 300, and that class was not injected.
Koi Fish account for $9{,}683$ triggers, of which $13\%$ exceed SNR 300.
The $60\%$ collapse rate above SNR 300 in Table~\ref{tab:real} is $9/15$ cells from five Koi-Fish triggers, not a rate for the catalogue tail.
The synthetic grid at $\rho_w\sim10^{3}$--$10^{4}$ probes a loudness that $5$--$10\%$ of the catalogue reaches, but the morphology that dominates that tail remains untested.

\subsection{Qualitative failure and recovery}
\label{sec:results-qual}

Fig.~\ref{fig:example} illustrates a representative H1 sine-Gaussian injection into the GW170817 package.
The whitened spectrogram shows a compact excess; the detector marks a contiguous time support; Tukey gates zero that support; and the matched-delta rebuild restores a frequency-domain package on which \DINGOBNS again yields an extended $\dL$ marginal.
Fig.~\ref{fig:posteriors} contrasts clean, poisoned, and gated one-dimensional posteriors in the official-control case study (an H1 sine-Gaussian at $f_0=100\,\mathrm{Hz}$, $Q=5$, $1\,\mathrm{s}$ before the trigger, $\rho_w=4.3\times10^{3}$).
The clean and gated posteriors shown, and those compared in Table~\ref{tab:js}, are both importance-sampled with the release's own machinery ($2\times10^{4}$ samples each); the poisoned posterior is the raw network output, since importance sampling of a posterior that has collapsed onto the prior boundary has negligible efficiency and would only confirm the collapse.
The poisoned run collapses to $\dL\approx10\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ with a 90\% interval of width $0.65\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, against a clean interval spanning $19$--$45\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
The gated run overlaps the clean reference on every one of the 15 sampled parameters (Table~\ref{tab:js}); the largest JS divergence, $0.012\,\mathrm{nat}$, occurs for the secondary spin magnitude $a_2$, and the $\dL$ median shifts by $-0.3\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
The importance-sampling efficiency of the gated package ($8.5\%$) is comparable to that of the clean package ($11.1\%$), indicating that the rebuilt input remains close to the network's training support rather than merely producing a plausible-looking marginal.

\subsection{Localization versus extent: the oracle comparison}
\label{sec:results-oracle}

The centred oracle knows the injection time and still recovers only $71.3\%$, against $85.8\%$ for the detector-driven gate.

Those two families are 21 of the 34.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:results-ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} and Fig.~\ref{fig:ablation} compare nine rewrites on the same $N=16$ hardening cells (severities 6 and 10, identical seeds and gate masks, $n=512$).
The clean reference median is $34\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
\texttt{adapt\_full} (gate, matched-delta, original ASD) recovers $13/16$ at a median of $34.3\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
\texttt{dingo\_repackage} passes the gated strain through \texttt{data\_to\_domain} with \texttt{time\_buffer}$=2$, the single official cyclic shift, and collapses $16/16$ to a median of $10.6\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
\texttt{dingo\_repackage\_aligned} uses the same call with \texttt{time\_buffer}$=0$, so the shift is not applied; it recovers $0/16$, with the median at $82.6\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ and only $2/16$ inside the collapse criterion.
Linear inpainting before the same two conditionings matches those two outcomes ($16/16$ at $10.6\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ with the shift; $0/16$ at $82.5\,\mathrm{Mpc}$ without it, and none collapsed).
\texttt{fft\_replace} collapses $16/16$ to $10.0\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
Welch-ASD and no-gate arms recover $6$--$12\%$.
Full re-derivation therefore fails in both conventions.
The shift changes which prior edge the failure approaches.
Only the matched-delta correction of the original package recovers.

\subsection{Unsubtracted GW170817 L1}
\label{sec:results-rawl1}

The injections above are added to the LVK BayesWave-cleaned package.
The open GWOSC L1 strain around GPS $1187008882.4$ still contains the pre-merger glitch.
High-pass filtered above $30\,\mathrm{Hz}$ it correlates with the cleaned frame at $0.992$; over the full band the correlation is $0.025$, because the two products differ below $30\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
The detector on that raw segment scores H1 $0.53$, L1 $1.00$, and V1 $0.25$, so only L1 crosses the event threshold $0.73$ and one gate is opened.
Importance sampling of the cleaned reference ($n=2\times10^{4}$) is ordinary: $\dL$ median $34.5\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, efficiency $10.9\%$ ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}=2179$), $\log Z=-10932$.
The raw L1 package has efficiency $1.1\times10^{-4}$ ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.2$) and $\log Z=-83325$.
\texttt{adapt\_full} on that raw L1 has efficiency $5\times10^{-5}$ ($n_{\mathrm{eff}}=1$) and $\log Z=-154461$, and the unweighted network distance is $10.4$ / $11.4$ / $13.4\,\mathrm{Mpc}$, which does not overlap the reference.
$\Delta\log Z\approx7.2\times10^{4}$ between the reference and the gated raw frame.
Gating removes the transient the detector saw.
It does not make the uncleaned L1 spectrum match the cleaned-frame analysis ASD, and a time-domain gate is not a substitute for coherent wavelet subtraction when the low-frequency noise is non-stationary~\cite{Cornish2015bayeswave,Pankow2018mitigation}.

\subsection{Cost of a false gate}
\label{sec:results-cleangate}

On $N=1{,}000$ clean stationary Gaussian draws coloured by the on-source ASD, the detector fires on $46/1000=4.6\%$ of crops at the validation threshold $0.46$ (Clopper--Pearson 95\% interval $[3.39\%,6.09\%]$) and on $0/1000$ at the event threshold $0.73$ ($[0,0.37\%]$).
The two seeds contribute $26/500$ and $20/500$ at $0.46$, and neither fires at $0.73$.
Off-source GWOSC crops are a different population: $434/500=86.8\%$ fire at $0.73$ ($[83.5\%,89.6\%]$), because those segments are non-stationary relative to the fixed on-source ASD.
That rate is why gating is tied to an estimated $\rho_w$ rather than to every threshold crossing (Section~\ref{sec:deploy}).
When a gate is forced onto clean GW170817 data, $23/25$ cells still meet the recovery criterion.
The largest $\dL$ JS divergence is $0.24\,\mathrm{nat}$, against a clean-versus-clean floor of $0.04\,\mathrm{nat}$ at $n=512$, and it occurs when the forced gate straddles coalescence.
The mean absolute median shift is $3.8\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
That is a bounded cost, not a collapse.

\subsection{Runtime}
\label{sec:results-runtime}

Component times on CPU, 200 iterations after warm-up, are STFT $4.71\,\mathrm{ms}$, the network and dilation $1.96\,\mathrm{ms}$, and the matched-delta rebuild $10.15\,\mathrm{ms}$.
The front-end total has median $16.87\,\mathrm{ms}$ and 95th percentile $19.03\,\mathrm{ms}$.
\texttt{GWSamplerGNPE} takes $0.408\,\mathrm{s}$ for 512 samples ($n=200$) and $10.72\,\mathrm{s}$ for $2\times10^{4}$ samples ($n=10$).
The $3.16\,\mathrm{s}$ figure from the hardening driver is injection and file I/O, not the front-end.
The front-end itself is inside a sub-second alert budget; a $2\times10^{4}$-sample importance-sampling draw is not~\cite{Chaudhary2024latency,Morisaki2023rapid}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%% =====================================================================

\subsection{Why the recipe works}
\label{sec:why}

The collapse is a flow taken off the set it was trained on.
Distance, read from amplitude against the whitening ASD, moves to a prior edge and the interval shrinks.
Position in the segment does not matter (Section~\ref{sec:results-threshold}); where the power sits in frequency does.
Koi Fish put broadband power in the $30$--$300\,\mathrm{Hz}$ band that carries most of the BNS SNR, so they collapse at a lower $\rho_w$ than the sine-Gaussians and broadband bursts of the sweep.
Raw samples do not say this has happened.
Importance sampling does~\cite{Dax2023is}, and on the raw L1 frame the gated efficiency is $5\times10^{-5}$ with the distance still at $11.4\,\mathrm{Mpc}$.
Gating removes the time support.
Whether the network accepts the result depends on the rewrite.
A fresh \texttt{data\_to\_domain} package fails on both sides of the $+2\,\mathrm{s}$ shift (Section~\ref{sec:results-ablation}); matched-delta (Eq.~\eqref{eq:matched-delta}) only adds the gate residual, so the packaging convention cancels.
A Welch ASD recomputed on the gated segment encodes the gate and leaves the training support as well~\cite{Wildberger2023psd,Chatziioannou2019psd}.
The same rule applies to any amortized estimator: correct the packaged input, do not re-derive it.

\subsection{Failure modes}
\label{sec:failures}

The residual failures are gate extent, not missed detections (Section~\ref{sec:results-oracle}).
Scattered-light arches are long and low-frequency, so both gates leave power behind and the oracle recovers only $23\%$.
A wider fixed dilation would help those arches and would cut more signal from compact bursts, where the centred window already beats the detector.
The next gate should follow the measured excess, or hand the long low-frequency events to wavelet subtraction~\cite{Cornish2015bayeswave,Hourihane2022overlap}.

\subsection{Relation to likelihood-based mitigation and retraining}
\label{sec:compare}

BayesWave remains the right tool when a likelihood is available and latency is not~\cite{Davis2022subtract,Ghonge2024efficacy}, and retraining the flow is the right tool when the training stack is in hand~\cite{Sun2024ttsf,Xiong2025robust}.
The front-end is the remaining case: a frozen checkpoint, and a budget of tens of milliseconds.
A learned subtraction can replace the gate~\cite{Dooney2025deepextractor,Mogushi2021nnetfix}.
It would still have to be written back by matched-delta with the original ASD.
Long, coherent, or unfamiliar glitches still belong to a joint signal--glitch sampler~\cite{Malz2025joint}.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

The real panel re-colours an O3 excerpt into GW170817, so the glitch's own noise is gone; the noise-only control checks one side of that.
The loudest bin is five Koi-Fish triggers.
Extremely Loud, $89\%$ of the O3 tail above SNR 300, was not injected.
Sky location is fixed, so localization is untested.
Section~\ref{sec:results-rawl1} is the other boundary: a gate does not repair a frame that disagrees with the analysis ASD below $30\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
The panels do not importance-sample every cell~\cite{Dax2023is}.
Where they do, the gated efficiency matches the clean one.
The detector is trained on the held-in generator.
Times are research-driver measurements, not a deployed pipeline.

\subsection{Deployment considerations}
\label{sec:deploy}

For low-latency use the detector should run on streaming crops.
At the event threshold $0.73$ the clean Gaussian false-trigger rate is $0/1000$; at the validation threshold $0.46$ it is $4.6\%$.
Gating should be reserved for triggers whose estimated $\rho_w$ exceeds a few hundred, below which the network is robust and a gate can only cost signal.
Off-source crops fire at $86.8\%$ against the fixed on-source ASD, which is a reason to require that loudness cut rather than a reason to lower the threshold.
Gates should be broadcast to the event packager only when a trigger coincides with a candidate coalescence window, minimizing unnecessary signal excision.
Logging detector probabilities, gate windows, and ASD provenance alongside posterior samples would preserve data-quality audit trails analogous to those used in O3 glitch subtraction campaigns~\cite{Davis2022subtract}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%% =====================================================================

The frozen \DINGOBNS checkpoint is more robust to transient glitches than a naive stress test would suggest, and less robust than a low-latency pipeline can afford.
Real O3 glitches at native loudness leave the posterior untouched below Omicron SNR 100, and no synthetic or real glitch below $\rho_w\approx100$ collapses it.
Above a morphology-dependent loudness the distance posterior collapses to a prior boundary.
The $11\%$ of O3 triggers above SNR 300 is not that measurement: $89\%$ of them are Extremely Loud and were not tested, and the $60\%$ rate in the loudest injected bin is five Koi-Fish triggers.
Raw samples do not flag the failure.
Importance sampling does, and does not repair it.
On collapsed runs the front-end recovers $17/17$ (Wilson $[81.6\%,100\%]$), preserves $181/183$ cells and $60/61$ unique glitches under cluster resampling, and keeps all 15 sampled parameters within $0.016\,\mathrm{nat}$ median JS on the 17 real cells.
Matched-delta with the original ASD is the rewrite that works.
A fresh \texttt{data\_to\_domain} package of the gated strain does not, whether or not the $+2\,\mathrm{s}$ shift is included.
The same gate applied to unsubtracted GWOSC L1 does not recover the cleaned-frame posterior, because the frames disagree below $30\,\mathrm{Hz}$.
A fixed-width oracle with perfect knowledge of the glitch time recovers fewer cells than the detector-driven gate, so the remaining failures are a matter of gate extent, and a clean-gate control bounds the price of a false trigger.
The accompanying software and archived results make every number reproducible and the front-end available for integration into low-latency neural inference stacks.

\section{Glitch family parameter ranges}
\label{app:glitch}

Held-in families, used for detector training and primary evaluation, are \texttt{sine\_gaussian}, \texttt{broadband\_burst}, \texttt{whistle}, \texttt{scattered\_light}, and \texttt{glitch\_train}.
Held-out families reserved for generalization tests are \texttt{ringing}, \texttt{double\_blip}, and \texttt{narrowband\_tone}.
Each draw samples a relative peak time within the analysis crop, an in-band severity, and family-specific shape parameters (central frequency, quality factor, chirp rate, envelope duration, or inter-blip delay) from broad uniform or log-uniform ranges implemented in \texttt{adapt.glitch\_augmentation}.
Injections are added to a single interferometer's time-domain strain and propagated to the frequency-domain package with the configured ASD policy.
Exact sampling ranges are versioned in the accompanying repository configuration files.

\section{STFT detector architecture and training}
\label{app:detector}

The detector is a convolutional encoder over whitened spectrograms of shape $(B,C,T,F)$ with $T=32$, $F=128$, and $C$ equal to the number of interferometer channels in the stack; the spectrogram geometry (FFT length 2048, window 1024, hop 256 samples at $4096\,\mathrm{Hz}$, linear interpolation of the 1025 real-FFT bins onto 128 bins over $20$--$512\,\mathrm{Hz}$, and resampling of the time axis to 32 bins over the $\approx4\,\mathrm{s}$ crop) is stored in the checkpoint and read back at inference time.
A stem convolution (32 base channels) is followed by two residual-style blocks that pool only along frequency, preserving time alignment; a $1\times 1$ convolution then emits per-interferometer time-bin logits.
The final bias is initialized to $-4$ so that a randomly initialized network is silent on clean data.
Training uses binary cross-entropy with positive-bin weight $3.0$, Adam at learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 16, 100 steps per epoch, and of order 20--30 epochs.
Thresholds are calibrated on a clean-containing calibration set to a target false-positive rate of $0.01$.
The released checkpoint reports a best validation score of $\approx 0.77$ at threshold $\approx 0.46$; event-level analyses optionally raise the threshold (e.g.\ $\approx 0.73$ on the GW170817 grid) after a per-event clean audit.
